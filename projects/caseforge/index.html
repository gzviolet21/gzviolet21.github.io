CaseForge

A case-interview assessment that lets a company screen candidates the way a consulting firm would — timed business cases, auto-scored reasoning, and a recruiter dashboard that treats results as evidence for a human decision, not a verdict.

01 — Problem

Case interviews don't scale, and scoring them is subjective

The case interview is the standard way consulting-style roles are assessed — but it's expensive to run. Every candidate needs a trained interviewer for 30–40 minutes, and two interviewers rarely score the same answer the same way. That makes early-stage screening slow, inconsistent, and hard to defend.

The goal was a tool that could run the first pass at scale: give every candidate the same realistic cases, score the parts that can be scored objectively, and hand a recruiter a structured profile instead of a gut feeling — while keeping a human firmly in the loop for the final call.

02 — Approach

Score the reasoning, across five capabilities

A run puts the candidate through a business case — a client framing exchange, then eight scored questions with exhibits and quantitative work, closing on a one-minute recommendation. Every question maps to one of five capabilities, so the output is a profile, not a single number:

  • Problem structuringrubric MCQIs the candidate framing the problem the right way before diving in?
  • Quantitative reasoningnumeric ± toleranceNumeric answers graded within a tolerance band, so "5m", "5 million", and 5.0 all pass.
  • Data interpretationexhibit-drivenReading dynamic tables and pulling the right figure under time pressure.
  • Business judgmentweighted MCQChoosing the option that balances real trade-offs, not just the financial answer.
  • Synthesissemantic rubricShort answers checked for the presence of key concepts across many valid phrasings.
Design decision

Short answers are graded on concepts, not exact wording — the rubric accepts multiple phrasings of the same idea. Grading matches meaning, so a correct answer phrased naturally isn't punished for word choice.

03 — Architecture

A scoring engine that runs entirely in the browser

CaseForge is a single-page app with no backend. Cases, rubrics, and scoring all live client-side; attempt history and recruiter review statuses persist in the browser. That keeps it zero-cost to host and trivial to demo — anyone can open the link and experience both sides.

04 — The recruiter side

Evidence for a conversation, not an automatic reject

The recruiter view aggregates every completed assessment into a review queue: overall score, status, and a capability breakdown per candidate. Filters separate the recommended from those that need review, and each profile carries the integrity context and the delivery notes.

The framing is deliberate and stated in the UI: scores are signals, not verdicts, and video delivery stays human-reviewed. The tool speeds up the first pass and makes it consistent — it never makes the hiring decision.

05 — What it demonstrates

Product thinking in a hard-to-fake domain

CaseForge shows a full product built end to end: realistic domain content, a scoring engine with three genuinely different grading strategies, an adaptive loop, and a second persona (the recruiter) with its own considered interface — all shipped as a fast, dependency-free app you can try in one click.
