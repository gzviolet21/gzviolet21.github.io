SpecFlow

A document-control system that replaced a hand-carried paper flow between Production and QA with a shared digital one — where a new product spec notifies QA automatically, and a verdict routes straight back to the team that submitted it.

01 — Problem

Specs were walked across the floor on paper

On the original line, Production wrote a product's actual spec values on a form and physically carried it to QA. QA reviewed it, and if something was off, the paper made its way back with hand-written notes. Nobody could see what was in the queue, revisions got lost, and a spec could sit on a desk for hours before anyone noticed.

The result was slow QC passes and no shared record. The goal: move the whole exchange into one screen both teams trust — so a spec is never in a drawer, and both sides always know exactly what is waiting on whom.

02 — Approach

One shared queue, driven by notifications

Production submits a spec — product, batch, line, and the actual measured values. The moment it lands, QA is notified; when QA rules on it, the verdict and notes route straight back to the submitter. Every document carries one of three states that both teams read at a glance:

  • In reviewawaiting QASubmitted by Production and sitting in QA's incoming queue.
  • ApprovedQC passQA confirmed the spec — cleared, with a record of who and when.
  • Needs revisionback to ProductionRejected with feedback; Production revises and resubmits as a new revision, which re-notifies QA.
Design decision

The status lives on the document, not in someone's inbox. Because both roles read the same queue, "where is that spec?" stops being a question you have to ask a person.

03 — Architecture

A two-role state machine with automatic hand-offs

SpecFlow is a role-switching single-page app. A submission moves a document through a small state machine, and each transition fires the notification for the other team. State persists in the browser, so the demo needs no backend and anyone can experience both sides from one link.

04 — The other side

Both teams see the same truth

Flip the role switch and the same data reframes: Production sees what it submitted and how QA responded; QA sees what is incoming and acts on it. Counters at the top — awaiting review, approved, needs revision — give each side its workload at a glance, and the notification bell means neither team has to go looking.

That shared visibility is the whole point: it collapses the round-trip that used to happen on paper into a loop that everyone can watch in real time, so QC passes land faster.

05 — What it demonstrates

Workflow design between two real teams

SpecFlow shows the part of software that isn't about a single user: modelling a hand-off between two roles with conflicting views, encoding it as a status a machine can enforce, and wiring the notifications that make a collaborative process actually flow — shipped as a fast, dependency-free app you can try from either seat.
