Aptly

A test-prep platform for Indonesian job-seekers: timed practice papers in the format employers actually use for Management Trainee and ODP hiring, auto-scored with percentile benchmarking, plus a self-report personality module built with its own reliability guardrails instead of a fake precision score.

01 — Problem

Most test-prep content assumes the wrong test

Indonesian job-seekers preparing for private-sector Management Trainee and ODP programs sit SHL/Talent Q-style batteries — numerical, verbal, logical, diagrammatic reasoning, situational judgement, and a HEXACO-style personality questionnaire, mostly in English. Most existing Indonesian prep content assumes you're studying for the civil-service SKD instead, which is a different test with different scoring (BKN's +5/0/passing-grade rule) and a shrinking share of who's actually hiring.

Aptly leads with the MT/ODP battery, keeps the BUMN equivalent (TKD RBB) as a companion track, and treats SKD CPNS as the smallest, most secondary offering — the reverse of how most Indonesian test-prep sites are built.

02 — Approach

Timed papers that behave like the real thing

Each paper runs on a real countdown with auto-submit on timeout, the same pressure a candidate feels in the actual assessment. Every question ships with a worked solution, not just an answer key, and every completed attempt is benchmarked against other candidates' scores on that same paper — so "you got 13/15" becomes "you're in the 79th percentile," which is the number that actually matters for deciding whether you're ready.

03 — The personality module

Built to not overclaim what it measures

The HEXACO-style questionnaire was the part that needed the most restraint. Four statements per trait is enough to demonstrate the format of a personality questionnaire — not enough for a stable individual measurement. So instead of a fake precise 0–100 score, results report a coarse band (lower/middle/upper third), suppressed entirely below a minimum-answered threshold, with an explicit on-page caveat about what the number can and can't tell you.

It's also untimed by design — no countdown, no auto-submit pressure — since rushing a self-report questionnaire produces noise, not signal. Small decisions like these are the difference between a feature that looks impressive in a demo and one that's honest about its own limits.

Design decision

A band instead of a score, and a threshold below which nothing is shown at all — reporting less was the harder, more correct call.

04 — Under the hood

A real backend, not a static demo

Next.js 16 (App Router, Server Actions) over Prisma and a serverless Postgres database. Auth is self-owned — hashed session tokens, not just a hashed password — so a database leak alone doesn't yield usable credentials. The paid tier runs on Midtrans rather than a card-only processor: Indonesian buyers pay overwhelmingly via GoPay, QRIS, and bank transfer, none of which support silent recurring charges the way a saved card does, so Premium is a 30-day top-up rather than an auto-renewing subscription.

Content is authored as validated JSON, checked by a script that rejects a paper where one option answers more than half the questions or the longest option is suspiciously likely to be correct — the kind of accidental tell that makes a practice test easy to game instead of useful.

05 — What it demonstrates

Full-stack ownership, end to end

Aptly is the widest-scope project here: content modelling, auth, a payment integration chosen for the actual market instead of the default choice, and a feature I deliberately under-promised on because overselling it would have been the easier, worse decision. Live and reachable today, not a local-only prototype.
