Brew Notes

A mobile-first coffee journal built end to end — log a cafe visit in seconds, then revisit it through a feed, stats, and a map. A small product that touches the whole stack: auth, validation, storage, geolocation, and route protection.

01 — Problem

Capture the moment fast, understand it later

A coffee journal only works if logging is frictionless — you're standing in a cafe, phone in one hand. But raw entries aren't the point; the value is in revisiting them: what you rated highest, which brew method you keep coming back to, where you've been.

So it needed two things done well: a capture flow fast enough to actually use, and a set of views that turn a pile of logs into something you'd want to browse.

02 — Approach

One quick log, four ways to look back

A single validated form captures everything — cafe, coffee, brew method, ratings, flavor notes, price, photo, and optional location — and the rest of the app is about retrieval:

  • Quick logform + photo + geoReact Hook Form + Zod validation, a Supabase Storage photo upload, and optional geolocation capture.
  • Feed & detailjournalA journal-style feed of saved logs, each with its own detail page.
  • StatspatternsStreaks, average rating, favorite brew method, and flavor patterns derived from the logs.
  • MapLeaflet pinsSaved cafes rendered as map pins — a geographic history of where you've had coffee.
Design decision — mobile-first

Every screen is designed for a phone in one hand first, then scaled up — because that's where a cafe log actually gets written. Magic-link auth keeps sign-in to a single tap, and protected routes keep each journal private.

03 — Architecture

Next.js App Router over Supabase

The app pairs the Next.js App Router with Supabase for auth, database, and storage. Route protection gates the journal behind a session; SWR keeps the feed and stats fresh; Leaflet renders the map. It ships on Vercel.

04 — What it demonstrates

The full surface of a real app

Brew Notes is deliberately end-to-end: authentication, form validation, file uploads, geolocation, database reads and writes, route protection, and a responsive UI — the full workflow of shipping a product, not a single feature in isolation.
